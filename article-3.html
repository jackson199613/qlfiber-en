Why glass tempering lines keep coming back to metal fiber

Ceramic roller covers, toothed bar protection and hot bending mould covers all fail the same way: the fabric survives the temperature but not the combination of temperature, abrasion and marking.

A glass tempering line is an unforgiving test for a textile. The material has to hold 350 °C continuously with short peaks far above it, resist abrasion from glass moving across it, and — the part that eliminates most candidates — leave no mark on the glass surface.

What metal fiber changes

Because the fiber is drawn metal rather than a coated or metallised yarn, there is no coating to burn off or shed onto the glass. 316L is the standard material; FeCrAl is available where the duty is hotter. Products are heat-set, so under normal cutting and handling they do not fuzz.

Where it goes on the line

Elastic knitted sleeves in Φ35 to 60 mm cover ceramic rollers. Braided sleeves in Φ5 to 14 mm protect toothed bars and racks in chemical strengthening, where resistance to molten potassium nitrate matters as much as temperature. Woven cloth in plain 0.35 mm or twill 0.5 mm, up to 1500 mm wide, covers hot bending moulds. Tape, rope and damping felt handle the rest: suction cups, clamps, container pusher bars, earthing.

Trying it

There is no minimum order on most of these products, and reference FOB pricing is available — roughly USD 19.5 to 45 per metre for TGZ112 depending on diameter, and USD 1.05 to 2.55 per metre for TGBX113. A trial can be a single roller.
