Pure metal conductive fiber — anti-static and shielding performance does not fade with washing.

What this solution is for

  • Coated or plated conductive materials lose conductivity after washing and abrasion.
  • Conductive fiber disperses poorly when blended with ordinary fiber, and spins badly.
  • Shielding materials degrade in high-temperature and corrosive environments.
  • A stable resistance range is needed to meet the product standard.

How we work together

  • Quoted by specification, by weight or by the metre; small-lot trial spinning and trial weaving are supported.
  • Blend ratio, fiber length, yarn count and twist are set to your process.
  • If you need a resistance limit, state it at enquiry — fiber resistance at room temperature varies with the state of the stainless steel fiber, so we recommend testing samples before qualification.

What buyers verify

  • Whether resistance and shielding effectiveness meet the target, and the retention rate after washing.
  • Spinnability on your machines, end-break rate and weaving efficiency.
  • Resistance consistency between batches.
If your conductive fabrics lose performance after washing, the problem is usually the conductive layer, not the fabric. QL supplies 100% stainless steel fibers in every form you need — blended tow, blended yarn (0.5–100% steel content with aramid, polyester, PPS or cotton), chopped fiber for conductive compounds, conductive thread (1–64 Ω/m) and braided sleeves. Because the conductor is pure metal, anti-static and shielding performance does not fade with washing. We can blend to your ratio and send trial yarn for your machines.
