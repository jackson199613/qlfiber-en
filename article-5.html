Specifying a conductive thread by resistance, not by steel content

Blend ratio is a manufacturing parameter. What your product actually needs is a resistance per metre, held within a tolerance, lot after lot.

Enquiries for conductive yarn often arrive specified as a percentage of stainless steel. It is a reasonable starting point, but it is not what determines whether a heated garment reaches temperature or a shielded fabric meets its attenuation target.

What to specify

Resistance per metre, and the tolerance you can live with. We hold 1 to 64 Ω/m, set by ply count and core count rather than by blend ratio alone. Fiber resistance at room temperature varies with the state of the stainless steel fiber, so if your product has a hard limit, state it at enquiry and we will test samples before qualification rather than after.

The other half of the specification

Hand, drape and sewability matter as much as the electrical value in a wearable product. Blend ratios from 0.5 to 100% steel are possible with aramid, polyester, PPS or cotton, and the right ratio is usually found by trial-spinning two or three candidates and letting your own process decide.

Because the conductor is drawn metal rather than a plated layer, the electrical behaviour does not fade with washing, flexing or time — which is what makes the qualification worth doing once.

Values quoted apply to the stated test or operating conditions and are a basis for selection, not a performance warranty. Final suitability is established by a trial under your own conditions.
